What it still needs to become a comprehensive math engine

The implementation above is a foundation, not a universal solver. Its calculus and root-finding features are numerical approximations, and its equation solver finds a root only when the chosen interval brackets one.

The next modules I'd recommend are:

    Symbolic algebra: simplify expressions, expand polynomials, factor equations, and solve supported equations analytically.

    Linear algebra: matrix operations, determinants, eigenvalues, and systems of linear equations.

    Differential equations: numerical ODE solvers such as Runge–Kutta.

    Optimization: find minima and maxima subject to supported constraints.

    Arbitrary precision: calculate beyond the precision of standard double.

    Long-running computation: convergence tests, cancellation, resource limits, and logging.

For symbolic algebra, arbitrary-precision arithmetic, and more advanced mathematical functions, you can also integrate established libraries such as GiNaC or Boost.Math.

One key question: Do you want this engine to solve equations symbolically (for example, derive an exact solution to x2−2=0x2−2=0), or is your main goal continuous numerical computation with very high precision and long-running calculations?
